The Late RoomsSide A rooms, side B noir
Noir phrasing Noir room Envelopes

Built on AMB-1's ways: slow, sparse, loops of unequal lengths drifting against each other, a held layer, the bass on the root, long rooms. Side B follows Bohren & der Club of Gore, Miles Davis's Ascenseur pour l'échafaud, Badalamenti, Vangelis's Blade Runner, Chet Baker and Bowie and Eno's Subterraneans: each piece has its own chord progression drawn from the outline of theirs (D minor and C7 four bars each; IV–I then ii, III7, vi, vi°, ♭VII, V7, I; I, vi, IV, V coloured with sevenths and ninths; minor chords moving to distant minors; the noir line cliché), and the horns, cello and vibes sing in phrases: a motif that leans into a long chord tone, the motif again on the next chord, an answer that settles, a breath. Vibrato only on the long tones, late. None of their melodies. Noir phrasing A/B switches the same track, from its start and with the same dice, between the phrasing as it was (A) and after your notes (B): breaths inside the phrase (often after the first note or two, sometimes a bar or two long), fewer notes, the motif varied when it returns, answers that do not always fall to the root, Pine Lake's hook coming home, and Velvet Doom leaving its line cliché behind every other time round. C moves between the two on purpose over each four minutes: sparse at the start, the conversation filling in, a pulling back, the fullest, a coda; the lead's phrases answered by the Rhodes (in Last Ferry, the cello) with the lead's own last notes, lower; the brushes coming in and fading with it. Picking a track plays on from it in order, and playing again carries on where it stopped. Each track starts fresh in its own key, fades in, plays as written (song mode, if you turn it on, adds its arcs: channels coming and going in overlapping waves), and fades out before the next. Envelopes · shaped is an experiment on side B: the long notes breathe the way players hold them, and listen. Each note of the sax, trumpet or cello takes a contour from how it comes: rising, it is pushed out; falling, it relaxes; the same note again, re-articulated; the first after a breath blooms from soft. The loudest long notes reach their peak and then fade through the rest, and a held chord fades a little after its peak. The pads, choir and Neon Rain's swell take a new contour at every chord and every note of their own, and the brushes drift through them. They make room: when another instrument comes in, the leads and drones give way a little (the drones more for a lead), and a lead pushing out its own note comes back through; the bass and brushes keep time and are not made room for. No jumps: never more than about 7 dB down from the level as written and never faster than about 9 dB a second; a lead changing note crossfades (the last held a quarter second as the next swells in) and lets go at the end of a phrase as gently as written; Neon Rain's swell beats less against itself. Bass, upright, vibes and Rhodes untouched; about 1.5 dB of make-up so it plays as loud. Noir room switches side B between a shorter room and echo (the reverb dying away in under half the time, the echo's repeats fading in two or three instead of a dozen) and the long ones it had, which rang on for 10 to 17 seconds after the playing stopped. Levels are matched from measured loudness. Playing the keys while it runs takes channel 1 for a moment.
