Glag manual

Glag turns the SP-1 into a generative ambient instrument. It plays itself from the moment it is switched on: four channels, each holding one of sixteen voices, drawing on one scale and one slowly drifting chord, through a tape echo and one of four reverbs. You tend it rather than play it. This manual covers every control, every page and every light.

1 · The controls

Control map

The SP-1 has four faders, four track buttons with a light above each, a row of four play lights, PLAY, ••, a volume rocker and a FFWD / RWD rocker. What the faders and track buttons do depends on the page you are on. Choose a page to see the map change.

2 · Pages

Getting around

Glag starts on Home. Every channel has its own page and an extended page behind it. Harmony is one page for the whole instrument. Tap •• twice from anywhere to come back Home.

Faders pick up. When you change page, a fader does nothing until it reaches the value it would change. Move it and the play lights blink that value, so you can find it. Once it passes through, it takes over. The emulator draws this as a dashed line on each fader.

3 · In plain words

What each control does

Glag writes the notes itself. You don't play it so much as tend it: the faders set how busy, bright and spacious it is, and which notes it is allowed to use. You can't play a wrong note. Every fader is safe to move at any time.

Home: the room everything plays in

These four affect all four channels at once.

TempoF1 on Home

The speed of the music's pulse, from 20 to 120 beats per minute. It sets how often each channel gets a chance to play and how fast the arpeggios and loops move. Ambient music sits low: most pieces are between 48 and 86.

Try: pull it all the way down to 20 for long, slow, spacious music.

DelayF2 on Home

A long tape echo, as in Brian Eno's Discreet Music: a loop of up to four seconds, a bar long in most pieces, with two playback heads, one on each side, the second three quarters of the way round. Each repeat comes back darker and thinner, and a little wobbly, as tape does. Higher means louder echoes that come round more times. The loop's length follows the tempo, so the repeats land in time. All the way down turns it off.

Try: raise it with only Kalimba or Pizz playing, and listen to the plucks bounce.

ReverbF3 on Home

How long the space rings, from a few seconds to most of a minute. Which space it is (Hall, Chamber, Shimmer or Bloom) is chosen on the Harmony page. Each channel chooses how much of itself to put in it (Reverb send, below). Plucked and struck voices go in bright; held ones go in darkened, so drones never turn shrill.

Try: turn it right up, then tap FFWD to freeze: the space holds the chord for as long as you like.

DJ filterF4 on Home

A tone sweep over the whole mix. In the middle it does nothing (there is a detent). To the left it removes the high notes, so the music sounds muffled, as if through a wall. To the right it removes the low notes, so it thins out like a small radio.

Try: sweep it slowly left and back to fade the music into the distance and out again.

Channel page: how one instrument plays

Each channel has its own four. Character is the voice's own control; the other three mean the same kind of thing on every voice, though each reads them in its own way.

CharacterF1 on a channel page

The one control that is different for every voice: the thing that makes that voice itself. On Bells it picks the kind of metal, on Arp the pattern, on Drone the interval, on Shimmer the buzz. The Voices chapter lists all sixteen.

Try: change it slowly while the voice plays to hear what it does.

ToneF2 on a channel page

How bright or dark the sound is. Down is soft and round; up is brighter, with more edge and shimmer. On the FM voices it changes how strongly one wave bends another, which is where the brightness comes from.

Try: keep the bass and pad dark and give one bell or piano the bright tone.

LengthF3 on a channel page

How long each note rings after it is played, from a short tap to many seconds. On held voices it is how long a note is held before it fades.

Try: long notes with low activity give the most ambient sound: few notes, each one ringing for a long time.

RegisterF4 on a channel page

Which octave the instrument plays in. It has five steps: two octaves down, one down, home, one up and two up. Home is a sensible range for that voice, so Bass starts low and Bells high. It moves the whole instrument; the notes it chooses stay the same.

Try: put two channels with the same voice in different registers so they don't crowd each other.

Extended page: where it sits, and its own colour

Hold •• and tap the channel's own T to get here.

Echo sendF1 on the extended page

How much of this channel goes into the delay. The Delay fader on Home sets what the echo sounds like; this sets who uses it. At zero this channel has no echo, however high Delay is.

Try: send only one channel to the echo so it has a part to itself.

Reverb sendF2 on the extended page

How much of this channel goes into the reverb. Higher sounds further away and more blurred; lower sounds close and dry.

Try: keep the bass low here, or it turns to mud, and the bells and pads high.

PanF3 on the extended page

Where the channel sits between your left and right ear. The middle has a detent. Some voices are already stereo; pan moves their whole spread.

Try: pan two plucked voices opposite each other.

ActivityF4 on the extended page

How busy the instrument is: how often it plays a new note. Low leaves long gaps, high plays most beats. On voices that hold notes, such as Pad and Drone, it sets how often they breathe in and out instead.

Try: two busy channels and two sparse ones usually sounds better than four in the middle.

The channel macro: the rocker on a channel page

On a channel page or its extended page, FFWD and RWD work that channel's macro instead of freeze and slow-down (those are on Home only; on Harmony the rocker alone is kept for later).

MacroFFWD on a channel page

A slow, wandering hand on the channel's own controls. It glides tone, character, length and activity to new random places, and adds tremolo and octave leaps, each in the way that suits the voice: EP gets a suitcase tremolo, Pizz and Arp leap octaves, Bells change their metal, Shimmer's buzz wanders. Each FFWD tap adds a step, up to four; at full it swings twice as wide as at half, with leaps of two octaves. Every piece also hides a macro scene: on Home, hold •• + FFWD for 3 seconds to bring it in. Saving the piece (•• + RWD for 3 seconds on Home) makes the macros as they are its scene.

Try: one step on a single channel, and let the rest of the piece stay put.

Macro speedRWD held on a channel page

Each glide starts at 8 seconds. Hold RWD and they get slower, twice as long every second and a half, down to one glide a minute. The play lights fill as it slows. A double tap on RWD turns the macro off and puts its speed back.

Try: full macro, slowed right down: the voice changes so slowly you only notice after a while.

Mixer

Hold •• on any page and the faders become the four channel volumes.

Level•• held + F1-F4

How loud each channel is in the mix. All the way down silences that channel, which also stops it costing any processing.

Try: bring a channel down to zero and back up slowly, as a fade in or out.

Harmony: which notes Glag may use

Hold •• and press PLAY once. All four channels share one key and one chord, so they always agree.

RootF1 on Harmony

The home note of the key, in 12 steps from C up to B. Moving it shifts everything up or down together; the mood stays the same.

Try: change it between pieces, or step it up slowly for a lift.

ModeF2 on Harmony

Which seven (or five) notes the key uses, and so its mood. Some are bright, some sad, some dreamy. The table below describes each one.

Try: try Lydian for floating and Aeolian for melancholy, on the same root.

DriftF3 on Harmony

How often the chord changes. All four channels follow the chord, so this is the music's slowest clock. Left changes it every 4 minutes or so, which is almost still; right changes it about every 10 seconds.

Try: keep it low for drones, and raise it when you want a sense of travel.

MotionF4 on Harmony

How much the music changes by itself over time. Glag has a slow tide, a couple of minutes long, that makes everything a little busier and brighter, then sparser and darker. Motion sets how deep that tide is and how often the loops change one of their notes. At zero the music holds steady. Up high it keeps evolving.

Try: turn it up and leave Glag alone for ten minutes.

The four reverbs

On the Harmony page, hold •• and tap a T. The choice is saved with the piece. The track lights show which one; for Shimmer, the play lights show its interval.

Hall•• + T1 on Harmony

The brightest and largest: notes arrive a moment before their space (40 ms), it barely wobbles, and its lows ring on after its highs fade.

Chamber•• + T2 on Harmony

The closest-sounding: the space starts almost with the note, mid-dark, with a gentle movement in it.

Shimmer•• + T3 on Harmony

The tail climbs: what rings is fed back a step higher, again and again, as in the shimmer pedals. Tap •• + T3 again to change the step: an octave (open and bright), a fifth, or a minor seventh. Frozen, it rises on and on.

Bloom•• + T4 on Harmony

The farthest away: notes swell into the space late, it is the darkest of the four, and it moves the most.

5 · Colour

Distortion

Every click shows both settings at once: the type on the track lights, as a number (Chebyshev 1, Overdrive 2, Fuzz 3, Fold 4, XOR 5, read as in Reading the lights), and the amount on the play lights. A hold never also counts as a tap. Holding •• with the volume rocker can't power the SP-1 off.

Chebyshev

A harmonic exciter. It adds the octave and the twelfth above whatever plays, the same at any volume. It colours rather than crushes.

Overdrive

Soft saturation, warmer and denser at each step.

Fuzz

An uneven hard clip with a buzz.

Fold

A wavefolder: past the edge, the wave turns back on itself.

XOR

Digital grit: the sound crushed to fewer bits and scrambled against its own last sample.

Before, after, or the reverb only

Before: the instruments are distorted and the delay and reverb carry them. After: the whole mix is, tails included, ahead of the DJ filter, so the filter can tame a fuzz. Reverb only: the instruments stay clean and the grit lives in the space around them.

Each step changes the colour more than the loudness: amount 1 sits close to the clean level, and each step adds a little. While distortion is on, one or two fewer notes can sound at once, which keeps the SP-1's processor within its budget.

7 · Sixteen voices

Voices

Any voice can sit on any channel, and two channels can share one. On Home, hold •• and tap a channel's T to step it to the next voice, in this order. Every voice reads tone, length, register and activity the same way; Character, F1 on its channel page, is the one control that differs.

8 · Sixteen recipes

Pieces

A piece sets every voice, value, harmony and space at once. Hold •• and press PLAY twice for the next one. The first eight follow AMB-1's stock pieces (by Charles Vestal) in Glag's voices: its pad, bells, bass and pulse on channels 1 to 4, its settings mapped onto Glag's, with a reverb chosen for each. A layer AMB-1 leaves out sits on its channel at level zero, ready to bring in. The last eight are Glag's own. Glag starts on Garden.

9 · On, off, recovery

Power

The power gestures come from Wakes, the SP-1 base Glag runs on, unchanged.

GestureDoes
•• held 1.5 sOn. Nothing shows for half a second, then the track lights fill as it starts.

T1 + T4 held while plugging in USB-CThe bootloader, for flashing firmware at solderless.engineering. It lives below anything Glag can write, so it always works.

The USB console (any serial terminal) logs what Glag does: pages, pieces, voices, distortion, and every 5 s the audio load. Glag has not yet been run on an SP-1; flash it at your own risk.
